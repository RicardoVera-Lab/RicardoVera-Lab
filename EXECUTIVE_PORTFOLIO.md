## 01 · SIPER-AGRO | The closing figures do not agree

**The management problem:** inventory, purchases, sales, settlements and freight records can tell different stories. A difference is not a diagnosis until the underlying documents are linked.

**Case evidence:** seven explicit comparisons across four domains; **three documentary discrepancies** (stock **−2 TN**, settlement **−500 ARS**, freight **+200 ARS**), **two commercial price spreads** (**−10 / +5 ARS/TN**) requiring contract context, and **two numerical matches**.

**My contribution:** structured comparison logic, prioritization by decision area and an executive evidence plan for inventory, treasury, logistics and commercial management.

**What a company gains:** a disciplined closing review that identifies what to verify, who should own the question and what evidence is needed before adjusting records.

**Technical proof:** CSV records, Python decimal arithmetic, nine tests and a multi-version GitHub validation workflow.

## 02 · MBA-PORT-001 | More revenue. Less operating margin.

**The management problem:** sales rise while the operation converts a smaller share into operating results.

**Case evidence:** annual net sales **32,473,404.18 UM**; simplified result **8,211,449.91 UM**; margin falls from **28.35%** to **22.17%**. Cutting discounts by 15% improves the model by **114,563.18 UM** at stable unit demand, but turns into **−82,452.97 UM** relative to baseline when units fall by 1%.

**My contribution:** source reconciliation, price and discount sensitivity, operational pilot controls and executive decision gates.

**What a company gains:** a way to challenge an attractive pricing idea before turning its assumptions into a financial commitment.

**Technical proof:** four source CSVs, repeatable Python analysis, frozen checksums, automated tests and executive reporting.

## 03 · SIPER-X / Patio del Molino | A margin that cannot absorb much error

**The management problem:** management receives signals on stock, refunds, purchases, costs and customer behavior — but needs to know which questions should come first.

**Case evidence:** **4,444,400 UM** sales against **151,998 UM** simplified operating result (**3.42%**). Under an **8%** increase in declared purchases, holding other inputs constant, the model result changes to **−889.36 UM**.

**My contribution:** consolidated financial reading, evidence boundaries, prioritized investigative tracks and a four-week review framework.

**What a company gains:** a management agenda focused on validating economics before committing to price, staffing or inventory actions.
