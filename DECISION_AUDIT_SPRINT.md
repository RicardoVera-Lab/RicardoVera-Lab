# CIEC LAB — Decision Audit Sprint

## Before the commitment becomes expensive, attack the claim.

A Decision Audit Sprint is the bounded entry engagement for organizations facing a consequential technical, analytical or R&D decision.

The starting object can be small:

> **one claim · one decision · one failure mode**

The purpose is not to create a larger report.

The purpose is to determine whether the decision deserves the confidence currently being placed on it.

---

# When to use it

A Decision Audit Sprint is useful when:

- a technical claim is about to drive a large commitment;
- a vendor assertion is difficult to independently verify;
- an AI or analytics output is being treated as decision-grade;
- an R&D hypothesis is consuming time or capital;
- a model appears convincing but the failure envelope is unclear;
- an acquisition or investment thesis depends on a technical assumption;
- a scientific interpretation is carrying more confidence than the evidence warrants;
- internal consensus formed faster than the evidence did.

---

# What CIEC LAB attacks

Depending on the case, the audit may examine:

- hidden assumptions;
- evidence provenance;
- source independence;
- measurement validity;
- rival explanations;
- model failure modes;
- implementation gaps;
- missing variables;
- reproducibility;
- uncertainty;
- decision reversibility;
- evidence that would falsify the preferred story;
- evidence that would justify commitment;
- whether more analysis can still change the decision.

---

# Core deliverable

The output is a bounded decision package containing, where applicable:

## 1. Strongest defensible claim
What the available evidence actually supports.

## 2. Claim boundary
What the evidence does **not** justify saying.

## 3. Rival explanation
The strongest credible alternative to the preferred story.

## 4. Assumption map
The assumptions that carry the decision.

## 5. Evidence map
What is verified, supported, inferred, hypothesized, unknown or conflicted.

## 6. Failure modes
Where the current analysis, model, thesis or implementation can break.

## 7. Missing evidence
The gaps that materially limit confidence.

## 8. Mortal evidence
The observation or result that should kill or redesign the preferred thesis.

## 9. Minimum next test
The smallest test, data request, expert review or experiment worth paying for next.

## 10. Decision boundary
A clear posture such as:

- **CONTINUE**
- **TEST**
- **REDESIGN**
- **PAUSE**
- **STOP**
- **COMMIT WITH GUARDRAILS**

The exact language is adapted to the decision context.

---

# What this is not

A Decision Audit Sprint is not:

- a guarantee that the decision will succeed;
- a substitute for legal, medical, engineering or regulated professional sign-off where required;
- a claim that CIEC LAB has domain expertise it does not possess;
- a promise to confirm management's preferred answer;
- a generic dashboard project;
- a sales deck disguised as research;
- a universal certification of an AI, model or vendor.

Where external domain expertise is necessary, the correct move is to incorporate it rather than simulate it.

---

# What happens if the evidence is weak?

That is a valid result.

CIEC LAB can return:

- a positive conclusion;
- a negative conclusion;
- bounded uncertainty;
- a hard evidence-access block;
- a recommendation to obtain different evidence;
- a recommendation to stop spending until a specific uncertainty is resolved.

> **A system that can only say “yes” is not research.**
